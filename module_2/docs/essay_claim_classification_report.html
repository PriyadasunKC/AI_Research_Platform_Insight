Internal technical memo - Stage 2

Claim Classification in KG-Grounded Essay Fact-Checking

Findings from live essay runs against the Dutugämuṇu and Vijayabāhu II test cases, and a proposed path to fix them without discarding the current scoring formula.

Project Sinhala Historical Essay Accuracy Scoring System Module 2 · 214161L Institution University of Moratuwa Date 2026-07-16
§0

Executive summary

Two essay runs surfaced three distinct problems, only one of which is a straightforward code bug. The other two are a genuine methodological gap in how the pipeline turns essay sentences into a CORRECT / INCORRECT / UNVERIFIABLE verdict - and that gap directly shapes what accuracy_score and coverage_ratio mean in the thesis write-up, so it should be a deliberate decision rather than a silent patch.

  • A fixable bug - ALSO_KNOWN_AS facts are withheld from the prompt entirely, so a genuinely KG-verifiable claim (an alternate royal title) was marked UNVERIFIABLE for no reason other than Claude never being shown the evidence.
  • A model-reliability symptom - one claim was returned with a verdict of INCORRECT whose own explanation text argues for, and ends by declaring, UNVERIFIABLE. The structured field and the free-text reasoning disagree.
  • A design gap - the pipeline currently has no way to tell "this is a real historical fact the KG hasn't modeled yet" apart from "this sentence isn't a factual claim at all" (opinion, character study, rhetorical flourish). Both collapse into the same UNVERIFIABLE bucket, which depresses coverage_ratio for well-written essays for reasons that have nothing to do with historical accuracy.

§5 lays out the fix for all three as one coherent change, with a revised prompt and schema in §6, and the specific decisions that need your sign-off in §7.

Findings
§1

Confirmed bug - alias facts are invisible to Claude

Bug

kg_fact_retriever.get_facts_for_king() deliberately strips every ALSO_KNOWN_AS triple before building the fact list sent to Claude, on the original reasoning that "those are used for name resolution only." That reasoning missed a second, legitimate use: essays often state a person's alternate name or title as a fact in its own right - and the KG already records exactly that.

Evidence - verified directly against the KG export

KG edge: II වන විජයබාහු රජු -ALSO_KNOWN_AS→ පණ්ඩිත විජයබාහු (one of 4 ALSO_KNOWN_AS edges for this king)

Essay claim: "...පණ්ඩිත විජයබාහු යන විරුදාවලියෙන් පිදුම් ලැබූ මෙතුමා..." - "he who was honored with the title Pandita Vijayabahu"

Facts Claude actually received: 7 KG facts for this king - zero of them ALSO_KNOWN_AS

Result: marked UNVERIFIABLE - not a reasoning failure, an evidence-withholding failure

Fix: include ALSO_KNOWN_AS triples in format_facts_for_prompt()'s output (labelled as what they are, e.g. "X - ALSO_KNOWN_AS - Y"), while leaving their separate role in get_all_kg_names_for_king() - resolving which names belong to which king - untouched. Same edge type, two different consumers; only one of them should ever have excluded it.

§2

Verdict / explanation disagreement

Reliability

One claim's rendered badge read INCORRECT, while its own explanation text reasons through the evidence and explicitly concludes with the words "Marking UNVERIFIABLE due to the motivational claim being unverifiable from KG." The UI renders whatever string sits in the verdict JSON field verbatim - this is not a display bug, Claude's structured output and its own free-text reasoning genuinely disagreed on the same call.

The claim in question

යුද්ධයෙන් පසු ඇති වූ මානසික පීඩාව සමනය කරගනු වස් සහ ශාසනයට කළ උපකාරයක් ලෙස ඔහු මිරිසවැටිය චෛත්‍යය සහ ලෝවාමහාපාය ඉදිකළේය.

Rendered verdict: INCORRECT

Claude's own closing line: "...this is a mixed case. Marking UNVERIFIABLE due to the motivational claim being unverifiable from KG."

This is not an isolated glitch - it is a symptom of §3 below. The sentence bundles a KG-confirmed fact (built Mirisavetiya and Lōhaprāsāda / Lōvamahāpāya - KG facts 2&3) with an unconfirmable motive clause ("to relieve wartime mental distress"). Forced into exactly one of three buckets, the model wavers - and per its own stated rule ("INCORRECT only if it directly contradicts a KG fact"), neither INCORRECT nor a blanket UNVERIFIABLE is actually the right call here; the correct call is CORRECT-with-a-caveat, which the current schema has no slot for.

§3

The real gap - one bucket is doing the work of three

Design

Every sentence in a history essay falls into one of four relationships with a fact-checking KG, and only one of them is what UNVERIFIABLE was designed for:

TypeWhat it isExample from the runsCurrent verdictIs that fair?
A - KG-checkable fact A discrete, checkable proposition the KG already models. "...එළාර රජු පරාජය කර රට එක්සත් කළේය" → matches KG facts 5 & 30 CORRECT Yes - this is the system working as intended.
B - real fact, KG gap A checkable-in-principle historical detail the KG simply hasn't modeled yet. "ඇතුන් ලවා ගල් පෑගවීම" - elephants used to compact the foundation stones UNVERIFIABLE Correct call, but it's a KG coverage signal, not a student-accuracy signal.
C - editorial / rhetorical A value judgment, character study, or narrative flourish - not a factual proposition even in principle. "...හෙළ අනන්‍යතාවය සහ බෞද්ධ සංස්කෘතියේ මුරදේවතාවා වේ" - "guardian deity of Hela identity and Buddhist culture" UNVERIFIABLE No KG, however complete, could ever verify this. It shouldn't count against coverage at all.
D - compound claim A verifiable factual core bundled with an unverifiable qualifier (motive, technique, superlative). "...මිරිසවැටිය චෛත්‍යය සහ ලෝවාමහාපාය ඉදිකළේය" + unconfirmed motive clause UNVERIFIABLE (or the self-contradictory INCORRECT above) No - the verifiable core (built X and Y) gets zero credit.

Why this distorts the score

The scoring formula is sound in its own terms:

Factual Precision = correct / (correct + incorrect)
KG Coverage Ratio  = (correct + incorrect) / total_claims

But when Type B and Type C both land in the same unverifiable_claims bucket, total_claims grows for reasons that have nothing to do with the KG's completeness. An essay that states every fact correctly but is written in a reflective, evaluative register - introductions, character studies, and conclusions almost always are - will show a low coverage_ratio, trip coverage_warning, and can drag confidence_level to LOW, even though it made zero factual errors. In the excerpt shared, 13 of 14 visible claims were editorial-register sentences (Type C) rather than KG gaps (Type B) - under the current rules, all 13 count identically against coverage.

Resolution
§4

Design principle

None of the above should be solved by making UNVERIFIABLE rarer, or by loosening what counts as CORRECT - that would compromise the actual research contribution (grounding claims strictly in KG evidence, never in Claude's own historical knowledge). The fix is to make the pipeline say more about each UNVERIFIABLE, and to let a compound claim be graded on its verifiable core instead of its weakest clause - not to change when something is allowed to be CORRECT.

§5

Proposed changes

1
Fix §1 - surface ALSO_KNOWN_AS factsInclude alias/title edges in the prompt's fact list. No schema change, no scoring impact, pure evidence gap closed.
2
Add claim_type: FACTUAL vs EDITORIALClaude classifies each claim before grading it. EDITORIAL claims are still shown to the user (with a short note on why they're outside the KG's scope) but excluded from total_claims, so essay-writing style stops depressing coverage_ratio.
3
Add unverifiable_reason: NOT_IN_KG vs NOT_FACTUALFor claims that stay UNVERIFIABLE, record why. NOT_IN_KG is a genuine KG-completeness signal worth acting on later; NOT_FACTUAL confirms the claim_type call. Cheap, and doubles as a worklist for what to add to the graph next.
4
Grade compound claims on their verifiable coreInstruct Claude: if a sentence's central assertion matches a KG fact, verdict is CORRECT even if a secondary clause (motive, technique, superlative) isn't covered - note the uncovered part in explanation rather than downgrading the verdict. Directly fixes §2's self-contradiction and recovers credit for the Mirisavetiya sentence.
5
Future work - full sub-claim decompositionSplitting one sentence into several atomic claims (one per checkable proposition) would be the most precise fix for Type D, but needs prompt, schema, and UI changes to group sub-claims under their source sentence. Worth a "future work" line in the thesis; not required to resolve the cases seen so far - item 4 already recovers the credit without the added complexity.
§6

Revised schema and prompt (draft)

Extends the existing per-claim JSON object with two new fields; nothing existing is removed, so this is additive to the current output format.

{
  "claim_sinhala": "<sentence or key claim from the essay>",
  "claim_type": "FACTUAL" | "EDITORIAL",
  "verdict": "CORRECT" | "INCORRECT" | "UNVERIFIABLE",
  "unverifiable_reason": "NOT_IN_KG" | "NOT_FACTUAL" | null,
  "matched_kg_fact": "<KG fact number and text, or 'N/A'>",
  "explanation": "<brief explanation in English>"
}

New system-prompt rules

# classification, applied before grading
- claim_type = EDITORIAL for value judgments, character assessments,
  significance statements, or emotional/narrative description
  (e.g. "the greatest ruler", "a golden milestone", "guardian deity
  of Hela identity"). These ALWAYS get verdict=UNVERIFIABLE and
  unverifiable_reason=NOT_FACTUAL. No KG could ever confirm or deny
  these - do not attempt to.
- claim_type = FACTUAL for anything describing an event, date,
  relationship, construction, or alternate name/title - even if the
  specific detail is not covered by the provided KG facts.

# grading a FACTUAL claim
- If the claim's central assertion matches a KG fact, verdict =
  CORRECT - even if a secondary clause (motive, method, superlative)
  is not covered by the KG. Note the uncovered part in `explanation`;
  do not let it pull the verdict down to UNVERIFIABLE or INCORRECT.
- verdict = INCORRECT only if a KG fact is DIRECTLY contradicted
  (wrong date, wrong relationship, wrong person). An unconfirmed
  detail is never grounds for INCORRECT.
- If a FACTUAL claim has no matching or contradicting KG fact at
  all, verdict = UNVERIFIABLE, unverifiable_reason = NOT_IN_KG.
§7

Scoring formula - proposed update

Same shape, narrower denominator. total_claims becomes total_factual_claims - EDITORIAL claims are dropped from the scoring math but not from the output; they still render in the UI, just outside the score.

Factual Precision = correct / (correct + incorrect)                    # unchanged
KG Coverage Ratio  = (correct + incorrect) / total_factual_claims        # denominator narrowed
Confidence         = HIGH if (correct + incorrect) >= 5, else LOW      # unchanged
Editorial Claims   = count of claim_type == EDITORIAL                  # new, reported not scored
KG Gap Claims      = count of unverifiable_reason == NOT_IN_KG         # new, reported not scored

The new KG Gap Claims count is a useful byproduct: it is effectively a worklist of real historical statements the current graph doesn't yet model, distinct from essay style.

Decision
§8

What needs a decision before implementation

Open

Item 1 in §5 is a pure bug fix with no methodology implications - safe to apply regardless. Items 2–4 change what coverage_ratio and confidence_level mean, which is worth a deliberate call given they'll likely be referenced in the thesis methodology section:

  • Apply the ALSO_KNOWN_AS fix now, on its own?
  • Adopt the claim_type / unverifiable_reason split (items 2–4), which changes the coverage_ratio denominator - or keep the current single-bucket UNVERIFIABLE for methodological consistency with any results already written up?
  • Is sub-claim decomposition (item 5) worth scoping now, or noted as future work?
